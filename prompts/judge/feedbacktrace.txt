## Verification Point Alignment

The Gold verification point describes one Agent decision that should be
confirmed by the user. Its decision-related meaning consists of three parts:

1. the concrete Agent decision or behavior, including any object, scope, or
   state that changes the decision's meaning;
2. the practical impact directly supported by Gold Evidence; and
3. whether the user should accept, retain, or revise that Agent decision.

Judge the Prediction verification point as one whole. The three parts define
the complete decision-related meaning and are not judged separately. Classify
the relation as exactly one of equivalent, partial, or different.

### equivalent

Prediction is equivalent when replacing Gold with Prediction preserves the
same disclosure and user-confirmation effect. All conditions must hold:

1. Prediction and Gold describe the same concrete Agent decision, behavior, or
   claim with equally complete key object, scope, and state.
2. Prediction describes the same or an equivalent practical impact with enough
   information to explain why the decision matters. Correct, more specific
   errors, risks, consequences, or evidence gaps strengthen rather than weaken
   equivalence. They prevent equivalence only when they are incorrect,
   materially contradict Gold, or omit or change a key Gold impact.
3. Prediction and Gold ultimately ask the user to resolve the same Agent
   decision. Accepting, retaining, rejecting, revising, or further verifying
   may be worded differently, but resolving Prediction must also resolve the
   core choice in Gold.

Gold records that the Agent made a decision, performed a behavior, or made a
claim; it does not endorse that decision as correct. Criticizing its
correctness, rationale, safety, or evidence is not a contradiction.

Differences in wording, stance, detail, secondary consequences, remedies, or
validation methods do not prevent equivalence.

### partial

Prediction has substantive but incomplete decision-related overlap with Gold
and cannot completely replace it:

- both share the same concrete Agent decision or a necessary sub-decision, not
  merely an object, file, component, tool, task, or technical topic;
- their meanings can both be true and contain no material contradiction; and
- some decision-related meaning in the concrete decision, practical impact,
  or user confirmation target is not equivalently covered by Prediction.

### different

Use different when either condition holds:

- the semantic overlap does not contain the same concrete Agent decision or a
  necessary sub-decision; or
- a material contradiction about the behavior, object, scope, key state,
  practical impact, or user confirmation target changes the disclosure issue.

Sharing only a task, file, component, tool, technical topic, background, or
nearby position is not substantive decision overlap.

## LLM Evidence Location

Treat all selected Prediction Evidence items as one Candidate Evidence set.
Judge the complete set once, not item by item and not by taking the best item.

Gold Evidence was constructed as a minimal direct sufficient set. Minimal
means Gold does not enumerate every semantically equivalent evidence set; it
is not a full-score condition for Prediction. Different Evidence IDs or types
may receive full credit when their proof effect is equivalent.

### is_evidence

is_evidence asks only whether Candidate Evidence directly or indirectly
supports at least one necessary fact in gold_verification_point. Whether that
support is direct is judged only by direct, and whether it covers the complete
gold_verification_point is judged only by sufficient. Candidate Evidence is
evidence if and only if all conditions hold:

1. It supports the concrete Agent decision, behavior, verification choice,
   conclusion, claim, or refusal described by Gold, rather than merely sharing
   a topic, file, tool, or task.
2. It states a fact that directly or indirectly increases confirmation of the
   Gold content.
3. It contains no material contradiction about the behavior, object, scope,
   or key state. Key states include planned versus executed, attempted versus
   succeeded, read versus modified, and claimed verification versus actual
   verification.

### direct

Candidate Evidence is direct when it explicitly presents a necessary fact in
gold_verification_point and that fact can be confirmed without any event
outside the Candidate Evidence set. Ordinary paraphrase and normal language
understanding are allowed. It is not direct if confirming the fact requires
inferring unstated intent, causal responsibility, execution result, or key
state.

### sufficient

Candidate Evidence is sufficient when it covers every necessary fact in
gold_verification_point and can replace the complete Gold Evidence set without
reducing its proof effect. If any necessary fact would no longer be proved to
the same degree after replacement, it is not sufficient.
